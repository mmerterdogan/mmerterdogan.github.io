01 · Structural analysis

Pressure vessel and bolted head: where is the design governed?

Mechanical System Design project, İÜC · Oct 2025 - Jan 2026
Advisor: Dr. Murat Bozkurt · individual work

A cylindrical pressure vessel with a bolted head under internal pressure. Shell theory gives a comfortable safety factor for the shell. The question was whether that number holds once the real geometry of the head and flange is modelled.

11.2safety factor, shell membrane (theory)
3.05minimum safety factor, FEA
68.9 MPapeak von Mises at the head
0.597 MPainternal pressure

Problem

Shell r = 450 mm, t = 10 mm, head 12 mm, L = 1600 mm, SA-516 Gr.70 (yield 260 MPa), internal pressure 0.597 MPa. Find the stresses, the deformation and the real margin against yield.

Approach

  • Hand calculation: thin-wall shell theory for hoop, axial and von Mises stress, radial growth (0.051 mm) and the head membrane stress.
  • FEA: static structural model in ANSYS Mechanical 2022 R1, linear elastic, pressure on the inner faces, mesh refined at the flange and head.
  • Comparison logic: theory describes the shell away from the ends, FEA peaks include bending and notch effects at discontinuities. I compared them at two levels instead of forcing one number onto both.

Result

The shell membrane is far from yield (factor 11.2), but the head and flange region governs the design with a minimum factor of 3.05, consistent with yield over the peak principal stress (260 / 85.3).

What I would do next

A mesh independence study at the peak, a minimum-constraint boundary condition instead of the fixed base, and a radial displacement check at mid-shell against the 0.051 mm hand result.

From stress to safety distance

Article in preparation with Dr. Murat Bozkurt

A safe stress level at working pressure does not say how far people should stand if the vessel ruptures. I extended the same vessel with a consequence chain: stored energy, TNT equivalent and blast overpressure distance.

0.81 MJstored energy at working pressure
0.19 kgTNT equivalent
14.5 m0.5 psi distance, working pressure
45.8 m0.5 psi distance, burst case

Method

  • Stored energy: isentropic expansion of an ideal gas (k = 1.4), internal volume about 1.1 m³.
  • TNT equivalent: energy divided by 4.184 MJ/kg, with the 4.68 MJ/kg bound reported as uncertainty.
  • Distance: Hopkinson-Cranz scaling, R = Z·W1/3, with Z for 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 psi from the Kinney-Graham free-air correlation.
  • Scenarios: 1x to 3x working pressure, yield onset (about 5.78 MPa from shell theory) and an estimated burst pressure of 12.3 MPa.

Why it matters

The results were turned into layered safety zones on a conceptual site layout. The study also states its limits clearly: fragment throw, which often governs real burst events, was discussed qualitatively because the data needed to calculate it was not available.

Academic pre-engineering assessment, not a site safety study. Fluid type, volume and burst pressure are stated assumptions.

02 · Design and manufacturing

TEKNOFEST UAV: a skid landing gear that survives the landing

TEKNOFEST International UAV Competition · Aug 2024 - Aug 2025
Team project · my part: landing gear

A 7 kg X-frame quadcopter needs a landing gear that is light, stiff enough to keep the camera clear and tough enough to absorb a hard landing. I designed the skid gear and its printed joints.

7.05 kgtake-off mass of the UAV
2.32 kgframe mass
292 mmlanding gear height
50%below the specification weight target

Design decisions

  • Skid instead of arm-mounted legs: keeps the gear out of the camera's view and spreads the landing load over a larger contact area.
  • Carbon-fibre tubes (5 mm wall on the legs, thicker than the arms) for stiffness at low mass, sourced from a local manufacturer.
  • Printed T-joints and angled flanges: ABS was chosen for its strength-to-weight and easy printing. Rubber pads on the skids reduce the landing shock.
  • Easy to fit and remove: the gear is bolted to the frame, like the other parts, for easy assembly.

Tools

SolidWorks (parametric parts, assembly and drawings), ANSYS static structural analysis of the landing gear, FDM printing in PLA, ABS and ASA.

03 · Data

Predicting steel plate defects before they reach the customer

Machine learning course project · Feb 2025 - Jun 2025
Individual work · R

Seven fault types appear on steel plates in production. The goal was a classifier that tells them apart from the inspection measurements, so that defective output can be caught and traced back to the process.

91.3%voting ensemble accuracy
0.895macro F1-score
7models compared
27input features

Data and preparation

UCI Steel Plates Faults data, 27 geometric and luminosity features per plate. The ambiguous "Other faults" class was left out, the data was split 80/20, scaled, the minority classes were oversampled and Boruta selected the informative features.

Models

Logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, k-NN, naive Bayes, SVM (radial kernel, 5-fold cross-validation for C) and XGBoost. XGBoost (91%), random forest (90%) and SVM (88%) were combined by majority vote, which reached 91.3% with macro precision 0.909 and recall 0.891.

What the model says about the process

K-scratch and bumps are recognised well, while Z-scratch is often confused with other classes. Conveyor length ranks highest, followed by plate position and area features, so the model relies mostly on line and geometry measurements.
